Failures before payment submission retry after 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 minutes, then hourly. Retries stop after 24 hours or at the next regular payment time, whichever comes first. A full 24-hour window allows roughly 29 attempts including the first; request duration can reduce that number.

These are attempts for one scheduled occurrence. Once a payment is submitted, its outcome is checked before another payment can be considered. An uncertain or pending payment is not automatically sent again.

Recurring schedules do not replay every missed interval. An overdue allowance may make one overdue payment, then advance to its next future scheduled date. Retrying an older occurrence preserves the regular schedule.

Several overdue allowances can each attempt a payment when a fault is fixed. If that is unwanted, ask the server operator to skip missed occurrences before reactivating the scheduler. There is no global limit on the total amount across allowances.

The extension normally allows only public HTTPS LNURL endpoints on port 443. This prevents recipient URLs from reaching private internal services.

With split DNS, a public-looking hostname can resolve to a private IP inside the LNbits container. That can block a legitimate recipient. Ask your server operator to check the recorded error and configure only the exact trusted service if needed; changing the recipient or repeatedly retrying does not fix that policy.

The operator can set ALLOWANCE_TRUSTED_LNURL_DESTINATIONS in the container environment to a JSON mapping of exact hostnames to exact trusted private IPv4 addresses. For example:

{"payments.example.com":["192.168.1.89"]}

Recreate the LNbits container to load a changed environment. Only trust services you control. Wildcards, network ranges, loopback, link-local addresses, HTTP, other ports and redirects remain blocked. HTTPS certificate checks still apply. Invoice callbacks are checked independently and may need their own exact hostname/IP entry.
